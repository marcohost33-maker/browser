## Consequence for the project profile

The specification leaves "what is considered a match" of `spec_version` to adopters:
exact, or only the major version (`SPEC_VERSION` definition). The research POUF matches
**exactly**, so today metadata declaring `1.0.36` is rejected (`SPEC_VERSION`) although its
semantics are identical. A bump therefore changes exactly one accepted string, for
example in `POUF.specVersion` and in the fixtures, and no verification logic.

Recommendation, in line with the review position "no silent specification bump inside a
security fix":

1. Keep `1.0.35` in every security fix, including #58.
2. Land the bump as its own change (#49, rebased onto `main`), with this review as its
   evidence. Re-run the python-tuf differential, which pins `tuf==7.0.1`, after the bump.
3. Keep exact matching. Major-only matching would accept every future 1.x minor or patch
   version without review, and that contradicts the pinned-profile approach of ADR-009.
